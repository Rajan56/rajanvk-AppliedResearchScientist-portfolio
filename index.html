1 · The research framework

Technology does not improve sustainability by itself. Management practice carries the effect.

This is the conceptual framework of my dissertation. Novel technologies, working inside a digital transformation, enable an enhanced form of performance management. That enhanced practice is the route to business, environmental and social results. Select any block to see what it means and which study supports it.

Scientific note. Enhanced performance management is the integrating construct of the framework. It was built from theory and from the pattern across four studies. The survey study tested a neighbouring mediator, business sustainability capability, and the two should not be read as the same variable. Source: V K (2026), doctoral dissertation, LUT University, section 2.4.

Model assumptions and limits

The line is a teaching model. Its parameters are illustrative and were not measured in any company. It shows a mechanism and a way of measuring, and it should not be read as a forecast for a real plant. The mechanisms follow what the case companies described in my studies: condition data shortens fault location, prediction replaces calendar maintenance, and simulation removes waste before it happens.

Digital twins for sustainable performance in Industry 5.0: a multiple-case study

Three companies, seven interviews, thirteen company documents, analysed with reflexive thematic analysis and a cross-case comparison. The study asked how digital twins contribute to sustainable performance, which challenges firms meet, and which technology synergies matter from a human-centric view.

4 · Survey evidence, made testable

Smart technologies improve environmental results only through business sustainability capability

In a survey of 179 Finnish SMEs, analysed with covariance-based structural equation modelling, the direct path from smart technologies to environmental sustainability was not significant. The indirect path through business sustainability was. Below you can generate synthetic firms with the published path values, change the paths, and see when the indirect effect can be told apart from zero.

What is real and what is simulated. The table gives the published estimates. The points and the live estimates come from synthetic data generated in the browser from those path values, with noise set so that the uncertainty at n = 179 is close to the published standard errors. Each construct is treated as one observed score, so the simulator illustrates the logic of mediation and does not reproduce the structural equation model. No survey response is stored or shown here. Source: V K, Saunila, Rantala and Ukko (2025), Corporate Social Responsibility and Environmental Management, 32(1), 835–848, open access. doi.org/10.1002/csr.2966

Power check in Python. In 300 replications with the published path values, a bootstrap test found the indirect effect in 38 % of samples at n = 100, in 61 % at n = 179 and in 92 % at n = 400 (python/mediation_check.py in the repository). A weak first path needs a large sample, which is worth knowing before a follow-up survey is designed.

Why this matters for a funded project. If the effect runs through capability, a project that counts installed sensors will report success long before any environmental result exists. The impact framework has to measure the capability step as well: how the firm plans, decides and follows up with the new data.
